# 1. Introduction
Welcome adventurers! The goal of this document is to translate Land Of Eem into an enjoyable play-by-post experience. I am always open to suggestions and improvements so please feel free to talk to me or post something in the suggestions forum. 
# 2. Definitions
Some definitions will change due to the fact that the rules were written with the intention of the game being run in real time. I will be referring to the original page of the rule and how I am changing it (again changes will most likely be made once playtested more and after receiving good feedback). 
## 2.1. What is considered to be a “Session”?
We will be considering a Session to be a week in real-time (subject to change). 
Here is a list of affected mechanics:
- Abilities that are based on “Session” ( pg 20, CRB )
- Gaining XP ( pg 22, CRB )
- Spending XP will be done during downtime since play is continuous ( pg 22, CRB )
- Crafting will be done during downtime since play ( pg 98, CRB )
- Item Traits that have “Session” effects ( pg 94, CRB )
- Identifying Magic Items & Relics ( pg 97, CRB )
- 1d6 Crafting Flaws that affect item for a “Session” ( pg 100, CRB )
- Makeshift Items that last for a “Session” ( pg 100, CRB )
- Components that have “Session” effects ( pg 102-116, CRB )
- Recipes that have “Session” effects ( pg 117-124, CRB )
- Defy Death ( pg 135, CRB )
- Adversaries that have “Session” effects ( pg 143, CRB )
- Perilous Encounters and Discoveries  that have “Session” effects ( pg 192 - 200, CRB )
- Magic Items and Relics that have “Session” effects ( pg 210 - 230, CRB )
- Boons and Curses that have “Session” effects ( pg 232 - 235, CRB )
- Spells that have “Session” effects ( pg 236 - 239, CRB )

Anything else that the GM comes up with that also has “Session” effects is also applicable.
# 3. Flow of Play
In this next section we will take a look a certain rules and mechanics and how we can improve them or expand upon them to ensure the following:
- The pace of the game doesn’t slow down or come to a halt
- Cooperative storytelling 
- Having fun
## 3.1. Cooperative Play
The great thing I love about this game is that it actually encourages the player to generate ideas and create outcomes instead of it always being the GMs job per say.

> *“Collaborate on interesting outcomes for dice results if the GM is struggling.” ( pg 12, CRB )*

And the same is encouraged for GMs.

> *“Work with the players’ ideas. You don’t have to say yes to everything, but always try to find a nugget to work with.” ( pg 12, CRB )*

The dice mechanics work well with this type of play because once a player rolls a die, they know the results of their roll already and what it means (unless roll is specified by GM). 

**The following flow of play is encouraged:**
Whenever a player rolls a die, they can determine the outcome on their own, ( ensuring the outcome fits the context of the scene ) and pass the idea to the GM if a second opinion is needed. Issues arise when players come up with an outlandish result that is only fun for them, and the outcome is out of context, or if it isn't true to what was rolled. There will be a fair amount of leeway and only the most extreme outcomes will need to be redone, it is mostly just common sense and discernment; this is not a strict rule, this is a way for players to be creative and cooperate and have fun. 

There are some exceptions to where the GM will determine the outcome of the result, those being:
- If a roll is given a Target Number ( pg 11,  CRB )
- If specified that a roll outcome is to be determined by the GM
- The result will be a Limited Outcome ( pg 11, CRB )
## 3.2. Scene Based Play
A scene begins with the GM describing the situation, location, etc. There will just be enough information to get the players thinking on what actions to take based on the GM’s prompt. A scene can be 20 posts or 100, there is no set limit to a scene and is ultimately up to the players if they want to keep a scene going or move on to the next one. A scene ends when all players are done interacting with the current situation and are now moving on to something different. For example: 

The party begins the scene after a long day of adventuring and now they are making camp for the night. They decide who will watch and tell campfire stories before going to bed. In the morning they pack up their things and venture forward to their next destination (END SCENE). 

The next scene would now begin with another GM prompt of the party’s journey to the next destination. The GM has two options, if the journey is to be significant then it would be a dedicated scene in itself, but if the journey isn’t to be the main focus then it could be put into another scene and just summarized.

The goal of a scene is to:
- Organize the flow of play
- Create a story/movie-esque feel
